> [!info] Genome-Wide Association Studies, or GWAS, is a major approach behind the advances in unveiling the genetic architecture for complex diseases.

- Questions
	- Explain in simple words how GWAS works. In particular, do GWAS identify causal variations? if not, what is the relationship between the "significant" SNPs and the putative causal variants?
	- GWAS is known to be inherently biased towards identifying common variants (minor allele frequency not too low). Why is that and what are the implications given what this article describes?
- Reference
	- <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5501872/> and references therein.
